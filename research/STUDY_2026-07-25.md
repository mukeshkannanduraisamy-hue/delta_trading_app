## 1. Conclusion of the exploratory phase (superseded — see §14)

Under the research process declared *before* the search began, **no strategy is
validated**. Two of 1,080 primary candidates cleared the Strict bar, but that is
not evidence of an edge:

- Both survivors are the **same strategy family** (`mean_reversion_bollinger`),
  differing only in timeframe.
- Both **collapse on the holdout**: development expectancy falls 65% and 98%.
- Every **gated variant died**. If the gates encoded anything real, some should
  have survived alongside their ungated siblings.

The development-to-holdout degradation is the central result of this study. It
is the signature of fitting, not of edge.

**Future work should begin as a new hypothesis with fresh data, not as an attempt
to rescue this outcome.** Re-slicing the surviving candidate under additional
protocols after seeing its result would convert the validation phase into a
second search — precisely the error the declared process exists to prevent.

> **What actually happened next (see §14).** That constraint was honoured. The
> surviving candidate was *not* retuned and its spent holdout was *not*
> re-sliced. Instead, ~435 days of BTCUSD history that had never been loaded in
> this project were identified, a single hypothesis was pre-registered in
> writing before that data was fetched, and exactly two candidates were tested
> once. The hypothesis was confirmed. The reversal came from a clean test, not
> from rescuing this result.

## 2. Methodology

**Signal on real spot, options as a calibrated overlay.** Delta purges option
candle history ~2 days after expiry, so a multi-month options backtest on real
option data is impossible. The study therefore signals on 365 days of real
BTCUSD 1m data (525,496 bars, 99.98% complete) and prices the option leg with an
overlay calibrated to the live chain and validated against real executions.

**How an option trade is modelled.** The live engine signals on the underlying
then buys an ATM option — a call on a long signal, a put on a short one. Both are
*long premium*, so both are simulated as long trades on their own premium series.
A fresh ATM contract is struck every DTE/2 hours, and **a trade never crosses a
contract roll** (see §9.1).

**Timeframes.** 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h — all resampled from the single 1m series, so no
timeframe can contain information another lacks. Resampling was validated against
Delta's native 5m candles (<2% divergence).

**Splits.** The last 20% is a final holdout, touched exactly once and only by
candidates that passed every prior criterion. The remaining 80% is development,
partitioned into 4 contiguous sub-periods.

> **Naming accuracy:** this partition is a *consistency-across-sub-periods*
> check, not a true anchored walk-forward — it does not train on window *i* and
> test on *i+1*. Because candidate parameters come from enumeration rather than
> per-window fitting, the check is still meaningful, but it should not be called
> walk-forward validation in the strict sense.

**Cost model.** Delta options: `min(0.01% × notional, 3.5% × premium) × 1.18 GST`,
read per-contract from `/v2/products` and verified live 2026-07-24.

## 3. Calibration and its limitations

The overlay prices with **Black-76 on the forward**, calibrated from one live
chain snapshot (456 quotes):

| Component | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Forward carry | 4.42%/yr (IQR 3.7–4.9%) | Put-call parity, 60 near-ATM pairs |
| ATM IV term | 0.22 (1–8h) → 0.34 (7d+) | Live chain, near-ATM |
| Smile | 1.03 ATM → 1.47 at x=−3 | 237 OTM-leg quotes, standardized moneyness |
| Spread | ~1.5% ATM (median 1.6%) | 456 live two-sided quotes |

**Credibility gate: PASSED at 11 of 22 contracts — exactly the threshold.**
Validated against *executable trade prints*, not Delta's `MARK:` series (a model
output that runs +72% to +439% above book mid for cheap OTM options).

**Limitations that every number in this report inherits:**

1. **The gate passed marginally**, not strongly. 11/22 is the minimum passing count.
2. **The real option window is a few days wide**, not months — a consequence of
   Delta's ~2-day history purge.
3. **The overlay validates unevenly by maturity**: 5/5 above 7d, 4/9 at 1–3d,
   only 2/8 below a day. This is why DTE ≥ 1d is the primary scope and <1d is
   exploratory only.
4. **Regime scaling was removed, not fitted.** One snapshot has zero variance in
   realized vol, so IV's response to vol regime could never be measured.
5. A single trade print lands at bid or ask, so the validation reference itself
   carries ±half-spread (~0.75% at ATM) of noise.

## 5. The Strict bar (fixed before the search, never relaxed)

Evaluated in order, short-circuiting at the first failure:

| # | Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `min_trades` | ≥ 200 trades |
| 2 | `positive_expectancy` | mean R > 0 |
| 3 | `walk_forward` | positive in ≥ 3 of 4 sub-periods |
| 4 | `shuffle_control` | beats direction-shuffled null, p < 0.05 |
| 5 | `corrected_significance` | Bonferroni over full budget, p < 0.05 |
| 6 | `overlay_band` | positive across ±15% IV error band |
| 7 | `final_holdout` | positive on holdout, touched exactly once |

The bar was **not** loosened after the overlay passed its own credibility gate
narrowly. A marginal overlay is an argument for keeping the bar strict, since
overlay error is one more reason a borderline result could be noise.

## 6. Results

### The two survivors

| Candidate | Trades | Dev expectancy | Holdout | Degradation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mean_reversion_bollinger` 5m / 1-3d / no gates | 3,637 | +0.1118 R | **+0.0025 R** | **−98%** |
| `mean_reversion_bollinger` 15m / 1-3d / no gates | 1,064 | +0.1568 R | +0.0534 R | −65% |

The 5m survivor's holdout expectancy of +0.0025 R is statistically
indistinguishable from zero.

### Why 2 of 1,080 is not evidence of an edge

- **Same family, no gates.** Two survivors from one family, both ungated, reads
  as one lucky configuration rather than a robust effect. Every gated variant of
  the same strategy failed.
- **Holdout collapse.** A genuine edge degrades modestly out-of-sample. A 65–98%
  collapse is the signature of in-sample fitting.
- **Selection is not covered by the correction.** Bonferroni corrected the
  *t-test* for 1,080 comparisons — that is the statistical layer, and it worked.
  But it does not correct for the act of *choosing which candidate to report*.
  Quoting the holdout of the best-of-1,080 is still a selected estimate, and the
  final interpretation must account for that even though the arithmetic is sound.
- **Base rate.** At p < 0.05 across 1,080 candidates, ~54 false positives would
  be expected before correction. Two survivors post-correction, both collapsing
  out-of-sample, is consistent with noise.

### The Strict bar did its job

536 candidates died at `positive_expectancy` and 410 at `min_trades`, but the
informative numbers are further down the chain: **19 died at
`corrected_significance` and 1 at `final_holdout`.** Those looked genuinely
promising in-sample and were rejected anyway. That is the bar working as designed.

## 8. Direction-drift analysis

BTC fell **−44.5%** across the window (dev −29.7%, holdout −21.0%). Because both
option directions buy premium, a put-biased strategy profits from that decline
regardless of signal quality — and the direction-shuffle null does *not* control
for it, since shuffling yields ~50/50 calls and puts, and any consistent bias
beats random in a trending market.

`research/drift_control.py` tests the question the shuffle cannot: force every
entry bar to a single direction, then compare.

| Candidate | Real | All-call | All-put | Edge over best constant | Call % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5m / 1-3d | +0.1066 | +0.0403 | +0.0116 | **+0.0662** | 52% |
| 15m / 1-3d | +0.1540 | +0.0883 | −0.0023 | **+0.0657** | 51% |

**The survivors are not disguised directional exposure.** At ~52/48
calls-to-puts they are balanced, and they beat both constant-direction
alternatives on identical entry bars. The per-bar direction choice does add
something over plain exposure.

**And it still did not generalize.** This matters: the drift analysis *cleared*
the survivors of the most likely confound, and they failed anyway. That
strengthens the negative conclusion rather than weakening it — the failure is not
explained away by a confound, it is a genuine failure to generalize.

## 9. Bugs found during the study

Both were caught by results that looked *too good*. Both are documented here
because fixing them **increases** confidence in the negative conclusion: the
final numbers come from a harness whose two known fabrication paths are closed.

### 9.1 Roll-boundary splicing (material — invalidated an entire run)

The rolling premium series splices consecutive option contracts end to end, and
the simulator initially traded straight through the seams.

| | Median | p90 | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jump at a roll boundary | **78.7%** | 4,609% | 4.4×10¹³% |
| Normal bar-to-bar move | 16.8% | 62.1% | — |

Rolls occur every 24 bars on 1h while trades could hold 48, so trades routinely
spanned two rolls. The extreme case is an expiring contract worth ~0 replaced by
a fresh ATM contract — booked as a price move no one could trade.

**Effect:** the first run reported 6 survivors with expectancy up to **+1.07 R
per trade at corrected p = 1e-83**. After confining trades to a single contract,
that candidate fell to **+0.178 R** and failed at `corrected_significance`.
Survivors dropped 6 → 2. The artifact was worth ~0.89 R per trade of fiction.

*Guarded by* `tests/test_roll_boundary.py`, including a test that drives a 10×
splice at every roll and asserts no trade can profit from it.

### 9.2 Direction mix mislabelling (reporting only)

`sim` labels trades by long/short, and both option legs are long premium — so
every trade including long puts was tagged `"CE"`. Direction mix therefore
reported 100% calls for strategies that were in fact two-sided.

**Effect:** P&L was unaffected, but the drift analysis in §8 would have produced
exactly the wrong conclusion — "100% call-biased in a falling market" instead of
the true balanced 52/48. Fixed by tagging the contract actually bought.

### Related fixes from the calibration phase

Each caught by data rather than reasoning: an unbounded error metric (divided by
a premium that collapses near expiry); a carry fit returning 0.687/yr against a
true 0.044/yr because deep-OTM legs polluted it; an ATM filter testing median
moneyness over a contract's whole life; and realized vol computed on a gappy
series, which annualized the gaps and moved the validation gate from 0/22 to 8/22.

## 10. Independent finding: option costs were materially overstated

**This finding stands regardless of the negative edge result.**

| | Previously assumed | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Taker fee | 0.03% | **0.01%** |
| ATM spread | ~15% (testnet figure) | **~1.5%** (production) |
| Cost per trade | 2.9 R (perp model) | **~0.1–0.3 R** |

The 2.9 R figure that closed the earlier study is the **1-minute perpetual** cost
model and does not transfer to options. With correct fees and real production
spreads, a 1-day ATM option with a 30% stop costs ~0.22 R round trip; at 3d with
a 50% stop, ~0.085 R.

**The strategies did not fail because options are too expensive.** They failed
because no edge validated. Expectancies clustered near zero rather than deeply
negative — exactly what a fair cost model applied to a coin-flip signal produces.
The cost objection was real but overstated; the edge objection is the binding one.

## 11. Recommendation (exploratory phase — see §14 for the current position)

**No deployment. Position size zero under current evidence.**

`sizing.recommend(..., validated=False)` returns zero by construction, so this is
structural rather than a matter of discipline. The engine should ship with sizing
disabled rather than with tuned numbers implying confidence the evidence does not
support.

The existing confidence-tier sizing table (>80 confidence → 100% of max lot at
20× leverage) should not be used. It encodes a guess at edge, compounded by
leverage, and this study found no measured edge to replace the guess with.

# 14. CONFIRMATORY PHASE — the exploratory conclusion is superseded

**Full protocol and result as recorded:** `PREREGISTRATION_2026-07-25.md`
**Code:** `research/confirm.py`, run once.

## 14.1 Why a confirmatory test was possible at all

§1 stated the holdout was consumed and no untouched data remained. **The second
half of that was wrong, and checking it is what changed the outcome.** Delta
serves BTCUSD 1m history back ~800 days; the exploratory study had loaded only
the most recent 365. Roughly **435 days had never been fetched, plotted or
summarised in this project**, and no selection of any kind had been performed on
them. That is a legitimate confirmatory validation set.

The assumption "we have no more data" had been inherited rather than verified —
the same failure mode as the MARK reference, the roll boundaries, and the book
recorder. The fourth time in this project an unchecked operational assumption
proved false.

## 14.2 Protocol

Written and fixed **before the validation data was loaded**:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | `mean_reversion_bollinger`, ungated, 1–3d ATM options, positive expectancy net of costs |
| Candidates | **Exactly 2** — 5m and 15m. No other family, timeframe, DTE band, gate or parameter |
| Data | Days 365–800, never previously loaded |
| Criteria | ≥200 trades **and** expectancy >0 **and** beats its constant-direction control |
| Failure handling | Recorded as failure. No re-slicing, no parameter adjustment, no third dataset |

Two tests, not 1,440 — which is what makes the outcome interpretable without a
multiple-comparison correction swallowing it.

## 14.3 Result: CONFIRMED

Window as found: **626,404 bars, 435 days, 2024-05-16 → 2025-07-25**,
100.0% complete, 0 bad OHLC bars, 0 gaps over 5 minutes.

| Candidate | Trades | Expectancy | Total R | t | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mean_reversion_bollinger` 5m 1-3d | 5,346 | **+0.0744 R** | +397.9 | 4.49 | 7.0e-06 |
| `mean_reversion_bollinger` 15m 1-3d | 1,562 | **+0.1391 R** | +217.3 | 4.79 | 1.7e-06 |

Both passed all three pre-registered criteria. (t and p are descriptive context,
not pre-registered criteria.)

## 14.4 The regime inverted and the edge held

| | Exploratory window | Confirmatory window |
|---|---|---|
| BTC | **−44.5%** | **+76.3%** |
| Call mix | 51% | 51% |
| Beats all-call | yes | yes |
| Beats all-put | yes | yes |

This is the strongest evidence in the study. §8 established the survivors were
not disguised short exposure in a *falling* market. §14 shows the same balanced
behaviour in a *rising* one. An edge that survives a regime inversion is not
fitted to regime, and is not directional beta in either direction.

## 14.5 Reinterpreting the exploratory holdout

| Candidate | Exploratory dev | Exploratory holdout | Confirmatory (untouched) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5m | +0.1118 | +0.0025 | **+0.0744** |
| 15m | +0.1568 | +0.0534 | **+0.1391** |

The exploratory holdout was the most recent 20% — about 73 days, BTC −21%. The
confirmatory window is **435 days, 6× longer**. The confirmatory figures land
between dev and holdout, far above the holdout that drove the negative
conclusion.

**Revised reading:** §6 attributed the holdout collapse to in-sample fitting.
That inference was reasonable on the evidence then available, but 73 days is
short enough for an unlucky regime to dominate. The larger untouched sample does
not show the collapse. **The holdout was hard, not fictitious.**

What §6 got right stands: 2 survivors from 1,080, with in-sample figures up to
+0.81 R that failed, *is* mostly noise. The rejections were correct. The
mean-reversion family was the one real signal inside that noise, and a 73-day
holdout could not separate the two.

## 14.6 The unresolved caveat — and it cuts against this result

**The overlay is calibrated to a 2026-07-25 option chain and applied to 2024–25
options.** Declared in §6 of the pre-registration before any result was seen.

It is not directional look-ahead — an IV surface does not reveal which way BTC
moves — but it is a **level** effect, and it is **asymmetric in favour of this
result**: these strategies *buy* premium. If options in 2024–25 were genuinely
more expensive than the 2026 snapshot implies, the overlay underprices what the
strategy would have paid, and expectancy is overstated.

The magnitude is unknown and cannot be measured: Delta purges option history ~2
days after expiry, so there is no 2024 option chain left to calibrate against.
**This caveat cannot be resolved with historical data. Only forward collection
can settle it.**

Secondary caveats: single instrument; single historical window; overlay passed
its own credibility gate at exactly threshold (11/22); present-day fee rates
applied to an earlier period.

## 14.7 Current recommendation: size remains ZERO

Per §5 of the pre-registration, declared in advance: **a single confirmatory
pass on one instrument over one historical window is not sufficient basis for
risking capital.** `sizing.recommend(..., validated=False)` continues to return
zero.

What changed is the **prior**, not the recommendation:

| | Before §14 | After §14 |
|---|---|---|
| Status | No validated edge | One hypothesis survived one clean test |
| Next step | New hypothesis | **Forward-validate this one** |
| Size | Zero | Zero |

**The decisive next test is forward validation on the book recorder data**,
where options are priced by the actual observed book rather than an extrapolated

Until then: no deployment, no sizing, and **no further tuning of this
